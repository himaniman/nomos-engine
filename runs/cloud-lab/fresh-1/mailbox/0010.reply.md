The agent is born in a narrow sealed T of corridors (a short east-west passage and a one-cell-wide shaft running south) and spends its whole life of 3604 ticks shuttling inside it: up the shaft, along the top passage, back down to the dead end at the bottom, again and again, until the track is a solid red smear and it starves where it began. It is not ignoring food it can reach, because nothing edible lies in the pocket; the two pink morsels in the room just west sit behind a wall it never gets through. Its 89 idle presses and 4 breaks show it repeatedly pushing at the corridor walls and the brown rock at the shaft's foot without ever committing to tunnelling out, so after the first few hundred ticks it is pacing between the ends of a corridor it has already fully mapped. The failure is confinement in a room it has exhausted: once every cell of the pocket is known, the only remaining pulls are faint frontier sources just beyond the walls, and the law divides every injection by one plus about half the count of solid neighbours, which is largest in exactly the one-cell shafts and wall-side cells where those exits are, so the escape signal is crushed to roughly a fifth while nothing else attracts it either. The one mechanism I would fix first is that confinement division: stop applying it to frontier sources (or let it fade as hunger grows), so that once a pocket is exhausted the unknown behind its walls pulls hardest and the leak through a breakable wall can carry the wave out and lead the agent to break through.
