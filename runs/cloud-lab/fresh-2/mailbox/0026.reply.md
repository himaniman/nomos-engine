The agent is born in a tiny sealed pocket under the big rock mass, a cell or two wide and about four cells tall, with the brown rubble patch on its west side, and it never leaves: the whole 4000-tick track (464 cells walked, only 31 distinct cells, last 500 ticks inside a 4x4 box) is a tight shuttle up and down that pocket until it starves at the north end. It does work at the walls (14 breaks, 16 idle presses), but the effort is scattered: it chips one face, turns, chips another, and no face is ever pushed all the way through, so it opens no doors. Open ground and growing food lie only one or two cells beyond the south and east walls, and none of that ever reaches the wave. Inside the pocket there is nothing to smell or see, so its only sources are its own wall faces, and those keep handing the lead to one another. The fix I would make first is the futility rule. Right now it marks a face as spent rock as soon as its work saturates, even when that face is visibly chipping. It should mark a face spent only when pressing it gave nothing. A face that yields should keep its work and stay the committed source until it opens, which gets the agent through one thin wall instead of rotating among all of them.
